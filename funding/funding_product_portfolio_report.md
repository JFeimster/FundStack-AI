# Funding Product Portfolio Report

## Executive Summary

This report consolidates the provided funding-product dataset into a single structured reference document. The source inventory contains **59 active, published products** across **19 providers**. The catalog is weighted heavily toward **Business funding products (43)**, with additional coverage in **Real Estate (9)** and **Personal finance / startup-use products (7)**.

At the portfolio level, the strongest concentration is in:

- **Term loans**
- **Revenue advances / MCA-style products**
- **Mortgages / real-estate financing**
- **Revolving lines of credit**

From an underwriting standpoint, the book is centered on **Fair-credit and Good-credit applicants**, with a meaningful but smaller **Subprime** segment for fast-turn revenue-based products.

## Strategic Reading of the Catalog

### 1. Fast-Capital Layer
These are the products best suited for urgency, payroll pressure, inventory gaps, working-capital crunches, and operators who need speed over elegance.

Typical examples in this layer include:
- Merchant cash advances
- Revenue advances
- Short-term business loans
- Weekly-fee LOC products
- Instant marketplace / merchant products

Typical characteristics:
- Fast approvals and funding
- Higher cost of capital
- Heavier use of bank statements, revenue validation, or platform/API connection
- Often more tolerant of weaker credit than bank-style products

### 2. Structured Growth Capital Layer
These products fit businesses that are operating with more stability and can tolerate a more traditional underwriting process.

Typical examples:
- Standard term loans
- Business lines of credit
- SBA loans
- Expansion loans
- Equipment financing

Typical characteristics:
- Better economics than emergency capital
- More documentation required
- Revenue and time-in-business matter more
- More useful for recurring growth plans, expansion, equipment, and refinancing

### 3. Startup / Credit-Leverage Layer
This bucket is especially useful for startups, new entities, and founders with weak business history but usable personal credit.

Typical examples:
- Business credit card stacking
- Personal/startup term loans
- Personal LOCs
- 401(k) rollover / ROBS
- Unsecured business finance programs using strong personal credit

Typical characteristics:
- Often viable even with no business revenue
- Strong dependence on personal credit profile
- Best positioned as startup fuel, bridge capital, or early-stage operating liquidity

### 4. Real Estate / Asset-Secured Layer
This segment serves investors, operators with collateral, and project-specific capital stacks.

Typical examples:
- Commercial real estate loans
- Hard money bridge loans
- DSCR rental loans
- Rental investment products
- Equipment finance and trucking finance

Typical characteristics:
- Collateral or asset-based underwriting
- Lower emphasis on unsecured consumer-style risk modeling
- More specialized documentation such as appraisal, rent roll, invoice, spec sheet, lease, or scope of work

### 5. Platform / Marketplace Finance Layer
This is the most obvious niche moat for e-commerce, Amazon, Walmart, and platform sellers.

Typical examples:
- 8fig Growth Plan
- OnRamp Funds
- Payability Instant Access / Advance
- SellersFi Working Capital

Typical characteristics:
- API or marketplace connection driven
- Revenue-share, remittance, or factoring logic
- Often low or irrelevant FICO dependence
- Strong fit for verticalized landing pages by seller type

## Qualification Patterns Across the Portfolio

### Credit Expectations
The product set spans three major qualification zones:

#### Strong-credit plays
Usually around **680–700+ FICO**

Best examples include:
- SBA loans
- startup stacking
- unsecured lines
- personal term loans / LOCs
- HELOC / home-equity style offers
- better-priced revolving products

#### Mid-tier / fair-credit plays
Usually around **600–660 FICO**

Best examples include:
- equipment financing
- business LOCs
- term loans
- AR financing
- growth loans
- commercial real estate in certain cases

#### Subprime / revenue-first plays
Often **500–580 FICO** or no stated minimum

Best examples include:
- MCAs
- revenue advances
- working-capital products
- gig-worker / instant approval products
- some short-term or flex-rate products

### Revenue Expectations
A few recurring minimum revenue bands show up repeatedly:

- **$3,000/month:** gig / creator / very small operator entry point
- **$8,500–$15,000/month:** common threshold for working-capital and line products
- **$20,000+/month:** more structured business products and specialty offers
- **Profitability required:** SBA / bank-adjacent programs

### Time in Business Patterns
Common patterns include:

- **0 months:** startup-friendly / credit-leverage products
- **4–6 months:** subprime revenue products and gig-access programs
- **1 year:** common threshold for lines, equipment, and many alternative term products
- **2 years:** SBA, prime products, and stronger business-bank products

## Operational Risk Notes

This portfolio includes several recurring disqualifier themes that should be handled carefully in lead qualification and routing.

### Frequent Disqualifier Themes
- Open bankruptcies
- Tax liens
- recent delinquencies / late payments
- recent inquiries
- recent NSFs
- low equity / low LTV support in real estate deals
- obsolete equipment / salvage-title issues for asset products
- platform disconnection or account suspension for marketplace products
- derogatory marks on credit-dependent startup products

### Common Documentation Requirements
Across the full catalog, the most frequent required docs are:

- Bank statements
- Tax returns
- Credit report
- Paystubs / W-2 / income verification
- Appraisal / lease / rent roll
- Invoice / spec sheet / equipment documentation
- Marketplace or API connection
- 401(k) / retirement account statements

Operationally, this means intake should not be treated as one-size-fits-all. It should be segmented by product family.

## Closing Note

This catalog is broad enough to support several different front-end experiences:

- a borrower-facing marketplace
- a broker training / routing guide
- a CRM decision tree
- vertical landing pages by industry or borrower type
- a qualification-matching assistant / GPT

The biggest practical win is not merely having the offers — it is organizing them by **borrower profile, speed need, documentation burden, credit band, and capital use case** so routing becomes fast and intelligent instead of chaotic.
